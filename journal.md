# Reflective Journal

## 9/14/26
I found the process of creating a website with Markdown and GitHub both engaging and confusing. Markdown itself wasn't the issue, as I started using Obsidian this summer, which relies on Markdown files, so I was already somewhat comfortable with the format. Therefore, I found myself able enough to recognize most of the notation in the template README in the version-control-workflow folder without needing the cheat sheet too often. What gave me pause were the instructions and the template itself: I wasn't sure how much of the template I was supposed to copy, whether I could rearrange things, and so on. Though, I may be just overthinking it as I am generally new to this field. 

The most engaging part was understanding the workflow between VS Code, GitHub Desktop, and the GitHub browser, and grasping how each tool functions and how they relate to one another. I look forward to learning more about the concepts behind these projects and how they fit into this workflow. That said, I may be wrong in my observations, but I was surprised to notice what I perceived as a delay between pushing edits to my README and seeing those changes reflected on the GitHub Pages site. It took me a while to notice this; so, for a while I worried that I was doing something wrong or missing something crucial. Is that delay normal? Is what I'm noticing not really a delay at all? Admittedly, I am not a "programmer" at the moment; so, I do not yet have the logic to confirm this myself. That said, we never simply are; rather, we are always becoming. Therefore, it would be more accurate to say that I am not yet as well as constantly becoming this notion of a programmer. 

With that in mind, I would like my work in this class to instill affect in others, though I am not yet sure what form that will take. Still, I wonder how much programming ability I will need to commit to such a creative aspiration. That said, I also wonder what kind of creation I am capable of given the limitations of my knowledge. Needless to say, I look forward to that process of discovery.
